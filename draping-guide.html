How to Drape

Most draping comes down to three things: the right length of cloth, a fabric that wants to fall, and one fixing point that holds it all. Here is how to drape the pieces you are most likely to own.

Plate I

Test it in the shop: hold a corner and let it fall.

Choose a fabric that moves

Fluid fabrics such as silk, viscose, modal and fine wool fall into soft folds. Crisp fabrics such as poplin and taffeta hold a shape instead. For most drapes you want the first kind. A quick test: hold the fabric by one corner and let it hang. If it forms soft, rolling folds, it will drape; if it stands out in stiff points, it will not.

Plate II

Length for most looks: about 180 cm.

The long scarf

  • Loose loop: fold in half, loop once, leave the ends uneven.
  • Parisian knot: fold in half, put it round the neck and pull both ends through the loop.
  • Over-the-shoulder: wrap once and throw one end back over the opposite shoulder.
Plate III

Larger squares and rectangles, 140 cm and up.

The wrap shawl

Fold a large square into a triangle, place the long edge across your shoulders and let the point fall at the back. For a sleeker line, cross the fronts and belt at the waist. In wind, knot the two ends loosely at one shoulder.

Plate IV

Wrap from the back, finish at the hip.

The sarong and wrap skirt

  1. Hold the fabric behind you at waist height.
  2. Bring both ends to the front and cross them over.
  3. Knot or tuck at the side of the waist.
  4. Adjust the overlap so it does not open as you walk.
Plate V

Typical length: 5.5 to 6 metres.

The saree, simply

A saree is tucked into a waistband (usually of a fitted petticoat), wrapped once around, gathered into pleats at the front and finished with the decorated end, the pallu, draped over the left shoulder. The pleats are usually 5 to 7 folds about a hand’s width wide. Regional styles vary widely, and each has its own sequence.

Plate VI

Knots, tucks, belts and, only when needed, pins.

Fixing points

Every drape needs one place where it is held: a knot at the shoulder, a tuck at the waist, a belt or a brooch. Choose the fixing point first, then let the rest of the fabric fall from it.
