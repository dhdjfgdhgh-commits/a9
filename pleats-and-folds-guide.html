Pleats and Folds

Pleats are folds made permanent. They add movement without bulk and turn a flat piece of cloth into something that swings, sways and catches the light.

Plate I

Movement without extra bulk.

Why pleats work

A pleat folds extra fabric into a narrow space. When you stand still, the garment looks slim; when you move, the folds open and the fabric swings. That balance is why pleated skirts and trousers have stayed popular for so long.

Plate II

Know the names, then spot them everywhere.

The main pleat types

PleatHow it is foldedEffect
KnifeAll folds face one directionCrisp, directional, classic skirts
BoxTwo folds turned away from each otherStructured, full
Inverted boxTwo folds meeting in the middleFlat front, room to move
AccordionNarrow zigzag folds, heat setLight, springy, fluid
SunrayAccordion pleats fanning from the waistDramatic swing
Plate III

Where pleats start changes everything.

Pleats and the body

Pleats that start at the waist add movement to the hem; pleats stitched down to the hip keep the line slim and release lower. Fine accordion pleats skim the body and suit most shapes; deep box pleats add volume and look best balanced with a fitted top.

Plate IV

Gathers, tucks and cowls.

Folds that are not pleats

  • Gathers: fabric drawn up on a thread; softer and rounder than pleats.
  • Tucks: narrow folds stitched flat, often decorative.
  • Cowls: loose folds that fall from a neckline or back, usually cut on the bias.
Plate V

Heat and water can undo them.

Caring for pleats

Heat-set synthetic pleats survive washing well; follow the label and hang to dry. Pressed pleats in natural fibres soften when washed, so press them back with a damp cloth along each fold, or have them professionally cleaned. Store pleated skirts hanging, by the waistband, so the folds fall naturally.
